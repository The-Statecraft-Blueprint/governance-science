# Contributing to Governance Architecture

Thank you for your interest in contributing to the development of governance architecture as a professional field.

This repository contains foundational infrastructure: a Domain Specific Language (DSL), design pattern library, and reference implementations. The work is developed with academic rigor and professional standards.

**Current Status**: This is primarily a solo development effort establishing the field's foundations. Contributions are welcome but must meet high quality standards. Most development will be done by the repository maintainer for the foreseeable future.

---

## What You Can Contribute

### DSL Terms

Propose new terms for the governance architecture glossary or suggest refinements to existing terms.

**Requirements**:
- Term addresses a governance architecture concept (not policy)
- Fills a gap in existing vocabulary
- Has clear, precise definition
- Includes concrete examples from real governance systems
- Acknowledges trade-offs
- Follows the DSL template exactly

### Design Patterns

Document proven approaches to recurring governance challenges.

**Requirements**:
- Pattern must have at least 3 real-world implementations (known uses)
- Must follow the pattern template precisely
- Must include evidence of effectiveness
- Must acknowledge trade-offs and failure modes
- Must explain when to use and when NOT to use

### Reference Implementations

Document real-world examples of governance patterns in action.

**Requirements**:
- Actual implemented system (not proposals)
- Documented outcomes (evidence)
- Clear connection to documented pattern
- Analysis of what worked and what didn't
- Transferability assessment (what's context-specific vs. generalizable)

### Corrections and Clarifications

Suggest improvements to existing documentation.

**Requirements**:
- Specific, actionable suggestion
- Evidence supporting the change
- Maintains consistency with rest of library

## Quality Standards

All contributions must meet these standards:

### Academic Rigor

- **Precision**: Definitions are unambiguous
- **Evidence**: Claims are supported by data, examples, or citations
- **Logic**: Arguments are valid, conclusions follow from premises
- **Scope**: Boundaries are clear, limitations acknowledged

### Practical Utility

- **Applicability**: Practitioners can use this in real work
- **Concreteness**: Abstract concepts tied to concrete examples
- **Actionability**: Enough detail for implementation
- **Trade-offs**: Honest about costs, not just benefits

### Professional Standards

- **Neutrality**: Not politically loaded or partisan
- **Completeness**: Follows templates fully
- **Consistency**: Aligns with existing library
- **Accessibility**: Non-specialists can understand core concepts

## How to Contribute

### 1. Check Existing Work

Before proposing something new:
- Search the DSL glossary for related terms
- Review the pattern library for similar patterns
- Check existing issues for ongoing discussions
- Understand the scope and standards

### 2. Open an Issue First

For any substantive contribution:
- Open an issue describing what you want to contribute
- Explain why it's needed
- Provide initial evidence or examples
- Get feedback before investing significant work

**Why**: Ensures the contribution aligns with project goals and standards before you spend time developing it fully.

### 3. Follow Templates

All contributions must use the appropriate template:

- **DSL terms**: Use the term template ([`DSL/template.md`](DSL/template.md))
- **Design patterns**: Use the pattern template (see [`patterns/template.md`](patterns/template.md))

**No exceptions.** Templates ensure consistency and quality.

### 4. Provide Evidence

- DSL terms need concrete examples from real systems
- Patterns need at least 3 known uses with outcomes data
- Reference implementations need documented evidence of results
- Claims need citations or verifiable sources

### 5. Submit a Pull Request

Once your contribution is complete:
- Create a pull request
- Reference the related issue
- Describe what you're contributing and why
- Be prepared to revise based on feedback

### 6. Expect Rigorous Review

Contributions will be reviewed for:
- Template compliance
- Quality of evidence
- Logical rigor
- Practical utility
- Consistency with existing work
- Appropriate scope

Expect multiple rounds of revision. High standards are maintained intentionally.

## What Happens After You Contribute

### Review Process

1. **Initial Review** (1-2 weeks): Maintainer reviews for basic quality and fit
2. **Revision** (as needed): You address feedback and refine
3. **Final Review** (1 week): Maintainer confirms standards met
4. **Integration** (1 week): Contribution merged and cross-referenced

Timeline may be longer depending on contribution complexity and maintainer availability.

### Attribution

Contributors will be credited in:
- Commit history (automatic via GitHub)
- Contributors file (for substantive contributions)
- Specific documentation they authored/improved

## Realistic Expectations

**This is a long-term, rigorous effort.** The goal is to establish professional infrastructure that lasts decades, not to rapidly accumulate contributions.

**Quality matters more than quantity.** One excellent, fully-documented pattern is more valuable than ten partially-developed ones.

**Most work will be done by the maintainer.** This is realistic for establishing a new professional field. As the field matures and more trained practitioners emerge, contribution volume may increase.

**Standards will not be lowered.** The field's credibility depends on maintaining rigor. If a contribution doesn't meet standards, it won't be accepted.

**Feedback may be blunt.** Professional criticism helps improve work. It's not personal—it's about maintaining quality.

## Code of Conduct

### Professional Standards

- Engage respectfully and professionally
- Focus on ideas, not people
- Accept constructive criticism gracefully
- Acknowledge when you're wrong
- Give credit appropriately

### Academic Integrity

- Cite sources appropriately
- Don't plagiarize
- Acknowledge limitations of your work
- Don't misrepresent evidence
- Be honest about trade-offs

### Scope Boundaries

- This is about governance architecture, not policy advocacy
- Contributions should be analytical, not partisan
- Focus on institutional design, not political outcomes
- Maintain professional neutrality

### Violations

Violations of professional standards or academic integrity may result in:
- Contribution rejection
- Being asked to revise approach
- In severe cases, being blocked from contributing

## Why These Standards?

**Academic Credibility**: For governance architecture to be taken seriously as a field, the work must demonstrate scholarly rigor.

**Professional Utility**: Practitioners need reliable, well-documented patterns they can trust and apply.

**International Applicability**: High standards ensure work generalizes across contexts and cultures.

**Long-term Viability**: Quality infrastructure lasts. This is building for decades, not months.

**Field Formation**: Professions form around shared standards. This repository establishes what "good governance architecture work" looks like.

---

## Future Evolution

As the field matures:

**More Contributors**: As more people are trained in governance architecture, contribution volume will increase.

**Refined Processes**: Contribution workflows may become more sophisticated with established peer review.

**Specialized Areas**: Sub-fields may emerge with their own patterns and vocabulary.

**International Collaboration**: Contributors from different democratic contexts will enrich the library.

**Professional Association**: Eventually, a formal professional association may oversee standards and contributions.

For now, we're building the foundation that makes all of that possible.
