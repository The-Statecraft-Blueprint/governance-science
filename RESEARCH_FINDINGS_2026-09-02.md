### 1b. Two better citations exist, and both are in your room

**Peter J. May, "Performance-Based Regulation and Regulatory Regimes: The Saga of Leaky Buildings," *Law & Policy* 25(4) (2003), 381–401** — characterises the failure as **flexibility without sufficient accountability**, not performance requirements as such.

### 1c. Spec §I.3 overclaims, narrowly

§I.3 says there has been *"no equivalent effort aimed at statutes at all."* The **Foundations for Evidence-Based Policymaking Act of 2018** (Pub. L. 115-435) requires major agencies to maintain learning agendas, annual evaluation plans and capacity assessments.

**The narrower claim survives and should be stated instead:** FEBPA builds *agency- and program-level* evidence systems; it does not pair an enacted statute with a measurable objective and a mandatory outcome review. That distinction is defensible and the current phrasing is not. A reader who works in federal evaluation will know FEBPA and will stop reading at that sentence.

### 1d. Spec §I.5 needs one word of layer precision

§I.5 says New Zealand *"made this separation nationally in 1991."* More precisely: the **Act** created the architecture (Building Industry Authority, territorial authorities, private certifiers, statutory routes for approved documents and alternative solutions); the **Building Regulations 1992** contained the performance Code. It was a separation at the **regulatory** layer, not a legislature-states-objective / separate-body-designs one.

That is the Westminster question from last turn, answered — and the answer is the awkward one.

## Part 2 — The main finding: existence is not the binding constraint

**Both runs converged on this independently, which is the strongest signal in the whole exercise.**

Run 1: *"Neither system simply lacked an objective, a reporting channel, or a legal correction path. Those existed on paper."* Grenfell had a 1999 select committee warning, a serious 2001 test, overseas fires, the Lakanal House coroner's recommendations, and repeated later warnings — reaching central government, Parliament, industry bodies and regulators. Approved Document B could be amended without touching the Building Act. **The failure was failure to interpret, own and act.**

Run 2: *"None reliably compels correction when the evidence is politically inconvenient."* Sweden's Riksrevisionen 2026 follow-up: action taken on **78%** of 158 recommendations — and it still **could not conclude the underlying problem had been remedied in nearly half the cases.** Switzerland's evaluation clauses reliably produce *studies*; the SFAO's own review found use for statutory change in **9%** of cases.

**So slots 4 and 5, as I wrote them, test for the wrong thing.** They ask whether a capability exists. Every jurisdiction studied has the capability. What none of them has is an **obligation** — a named party who must decide, by a date, on a record, with a consequence for not deciding.

### Why this is a gain rather than a loss

Two reasons, and the second is the one that matters.

**First, it makes the framework falsifiable in a way it was not.** "Is there a correction path?" is answerable yes almost everywhere, which means the profile was scoring nearly everything as present and telling you nothing. "Who must decide, by when, on what record, with what escalation?" discriminates.

**Second — and this is the part worth sitting with — the GDA already answers the harder question.** Look at what the spec specifies:

| The operational test | Where the spec already answers it |
|---|---|
| Who aggregates reports? | §V.1–V.3 — intake, triage, routing, **pattern detection as error-signal manufacture** |
| Who must decide? | §V.10 — the **consideration guarantee** |
| On what record? | §V.5, §V.8 — options with stated trade-offs, published deliberation |
| With what escalation? | §V.9 — the **report card**, carrying causal content |
| What obliges any of it? | §IV.3 — an enforceable duty running **toward citizens** |

**The research refuted my measuring instrument, not your design.** The design was already built around obligation rather than capability; the five-slot profile I proposed had flattened that back into an existence test. That is my error, and it is the exact error §III.6 warns about — *every correction must pass through the body it would correct* is a claim about obligation, not about channels.

Run 1's own recommendation, unprompted, was: *"turn items 4 and 5 from existence tests into operational tests: who aggregates reports, who must decide, by when, on what record, with what escalation if no action follows?"*

That sentence is a description of the GDA loop, written by a researcher who had never heard of it.

## Part 3 — The revised conformance profile

Five slots, not six. Run 1 proposed a sixth ("continuing epistemic capacity") and then said in the next sentence that resources, incentives and accountability *"should be tested across all six rather than treated as another box."* It's right the second time — capacity is a cross-cutting condition, not a slot.

**Each slot now carries four questions: owner, trigger, record, escalation.**

| Slot | The obligation, not the capability |
|---|---|
| **1. Stated objective** | Is there an outcome with an affected population, a baseline, a metric, a time horizon, **and a decision rule for competing objectives**? Where does it live, and does that location bind? |
| **2. Checkable design** | Is there a public assurance case linking the design to the objective — **at two levels: the rule, and the instance applying it**? Who must produce it, against what alternatives? |
| **3. Checkable execution** | Traceable records, independent verification, controlled change. Who verifies, and can they be refused? |
| **4. Aggregation and analysis** | Not *is there a channel* — **who is obliged to aggregate, analyse, and escalate**, on what trigger, and what happens to a signal nobody wants to receive? |
| **5. Obliged correction** | Not *can the design be amended* — **who must decide, by when, on what public record, with what consequence for not deciding**? |

**Cross-cutting condition (tested at every slot, scored separately):** capacity — usable administrative data, preserved baselines, comparison groups, skilled personnel, funding, and continuity across administrations. Run 2 is emphatic that this is where the real cost sits and where evaluation regimes quietly fail.

**Slot 2's two-level split is new and it generalises.** It came out of the building cases (the code layer versus the individual building) but it is the rule/instance distinction, and it maps directly onto architecture-as-published-specification versus architecture-as-applied. Worth carrying.

**Consequence for the flags→slots mapping in the proposal:** it mostly survives, since the slots were refined rather than replaced, but it needs a re-check against the obligation phrasing before it is written down.

## Part 4 — Two things the research found that I did not ask for, and both are better than what I asked for

### 4a. ⭐ Robust Details — the positive control, in your own domain, in the same jurisdiction as Grenfell

England & Wales, 2004, for Part E sound insulation. The route combined:

- measurable acoustic thresholds;
- pre-validated construction details;
- **registration of each intended use**;
- random inspections;
- **post-completion testing**;
- and the ability to **amend or withdraw an underperforming detail without amending the underlying regulation**.

A government-commissioned three-year evaluation (Faber Maunsell/DCLG, *Robust Details and Robust Details Ltd: 3 Year Report*, July 2008, esp. pp. 5–6, 10–16, 25–28, 35–42) found it met its objectives.

**This is the aviation-against-itself comparison, relocated into the building domain.** Same country, same Building Regulations, same era, same constitutional system as Grenfell — one subsystem built the loop and worked; the other did not and killed seventy-two people. **The only variable is whether the loop exists.**

Three consequences:

1. **It is a within-jurisdiction control**, which is methodologically stronger than any cross-national comparison, and it does not require the NTSB recommendation-adoption rate that has been blocked for months. `RESEARCH_LEADS.md` §1.2 can be downgraded again.
2. **It sits in the domain where the framework's whole telling already lives** — §I.5's performance-based codes, §III.5's New Zealand, and the unified building metaphor. A listener crosses no boundary.
3. **It answers the objection the framework could not previously answer with evidence: *does the loop actually change outcomes?*** Previously that rested on aviation, unpinned. Now there is a bounded, evaluated, positive case.

**The honest limit, and state it first:** Robust Details is a bounded acoustic-compliance subsystem. It is not proof that the same arrangement controls catastrophic fire risk, and anyone who knows it will say so. What it demonstrates is that **feedback-capable performance regulation is possible within the same constitutional setting that produced Grenfell** — which is a smaller claim and a sufficient one.

### 4b. Estonia — the drafting-reform counterfactual, live and better-sourced than New Zealand

Estonia's *Rules for Good Legislative Practice and Legislative Drafting* require an explanatory memorandum to state a bill's purpose and impacts, and — where ex-post assessment is planned — to specify **an activity plan, an estimated date, impact categories and assessment criteria, or explain why evaluation is unnecessary.** The resulting report addresses purpose, attainment criteria, effects, stakeholder feedback and further action, and goes to the Government and the Riigikogu.

That is close to the framework's own proposal, written into binding drafting rules. **And the OECD assessment says implementation remains limited and quality oversight needs strengthening.**

**This is the "why not drafting reform alone?" answer, and it is stronger than New Zealand for that job** — because it is that experiment directly (drafting rules requiring evaluable objectives), currently running, rather than a building-regulation case that has to be argued into the analogy. New Zealand keeps its place as the *separation-without-a-loop* case; Estonia becomes the *drafting-reform-alone* case. They were doing one job between them and now do one each.

## Part 5 — Register entries, drafted

### `OBJECTIONS.md` — three new, all Tier 1

**◐ The existence objection.** *"Your framework says the channel and the correction path are missing. In every documented case they existed."* Grenfell: warnings reached Parliament, government, regulators and coroners; Approved Document B was amendable without primary legislation. **Status: conceded and absorbed.** The claim is restated as obligation rather than capability — who must decide, by when, on what record, with what escalation. **This concession improves the framework and should be volunteered, not defended.** (Source: Grenfell Tower Inquiry Phase 2, vol. 1 ¶¶2.4–2.18; Hackitt 2018 pp. 27–28.)

**○ Hunn declines the causal claim.** The authoritative NZ post-mortem found insufficient evidence that performance-based regulation caused the crisis (p. 41) and recommended keeping the performance Code. Anyone who knows the case will raise this the moment New Zealand is cited. **The answer is that the framework never claimed performance-based regulation caused it** — it claims the missing loop did, which is Mumford's and May's reading. But the framework must say that *first*, not in response.

**◐ "Recommendation handled is not outcome achieved."** Riksrevisionen's 2026 follow-up: action on 78% of 158 recommendations, yet in nearly half the cases it could not conclude the underlying problem was remedied. This cuts against any framework claim that publication plus a duty to respond produces correction. **It is also an argument for §V.9's report card carrying causal content rather than compliance** — which the spec already says, and can now support with evidence.

### `RESEARCH_LEADS.md`

**✅ 1.10 Swiss Art. 170 — RESOLVED. Neither dead letter nor transformative.** Art. 170 is an organisational mandate on the Federal Assembly, operationalised through Parliament Act arts. 26/27/44 and RVOG art. 5. The PCA runs ~3–4 deep evaluations a year on roughly eight professional staff. Rüefli's inventory: 1,414 federal evaluations 1999–2015 (~83/yr) with **no clear post-2000 growth trend** and heavy concentration in a few offices; major institutions predated Art. 170 (the PCA was created in 1990). Bussmann: mature evaluation culture, limited influence on strategic political decisions. Widmer: parliamentarians request evaluations more readily than they use findings. Art. 190 means a federal Act is not invalidated for ineffectiveness. **Cite it as constitutional legitimation of an existing practice, never as a working correction loop.**

**❌ Nordic preparatory works — the claim does not hold, and the disconfirming case is excellent.** *Förarbeten* record reasoning, problem definition and political accommodation; they do **not** systematically state measurable objectives prepared for later evaluation. **NJA 2016 s. 3 is the case to keep:** the government proposition said life imprisonment should become the normal sentence for most murders; the Supreme Court held the enacted textual amendment could not bear that change. **The objective was stated, authoritatively, in the preparatory works — and it did not bind, because it was not in the statute.** That is Part I's point arriving from the opposite direction, and it is a better illustration than anything currently in the corpus. Move to §3 (leads that paid off), reframed.

**⚠️ *Ministerstyre* — I overstated it, and the correction is mine to own.** Instrument of Government ch. 12 art. 2 bars directing an agency's decision **in a particular case** involving the exercise of public authority toward an individual or municipality, or the application of law. It does **not** make agencies generally independent — the Government steers through ordinances, instructions, budgets, *regleringsbrev*, appointments and assignments. And it dates from the **1974 Instrument of Government**, not from the seventeenth century; the older administrative separation is real but is not this rule. **It remains genuinely interesting** — a *regleringsbrev* is close to a written mandate, and the Police Authority's 2025 letter contains both an outcome ("crime should decrease") and an output ("at least 5,000 cameras by end-2027"), which is a live demonstration of exactly the gap §I.3 describes. But it is not centuries-old constitutional prior art, and I said it was.

**↓ 1.2 NTSB adoption rate — downgrade further.** Robust Details supplies a within-case control in the framework's own domain. The NTSB number is now optional rather than merely non-blocking.

**New — worth one pass each:** the UK's post-legislative scrutiny convention (Cabinet Office *Guide to Making Legislation* ch. 40 — departmental memorandum to the relevant Commons committee 3–5 years after Royal Assent); and the Netherlands' *Comptabiliteitswet 2016* arts. 3.1/4.1 with the *Regeling periodiek evaluatieonderzoek 2022* (300+ evaluations annually, but keyed to budget themes rather than statutes).
