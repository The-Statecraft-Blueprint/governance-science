A true story from Utah · 60 seconds

The 100% ID law

On January 1, 2026, Utah started requiring an ID, scanned electronically, for every alcohol purchase. Every customer. Every age.

The backlash came fast. Restaurants turned away whole tables because one person left their ID at the hotel. Tourists couldn't order a beer without a passport. Retirees got carded for the first time in decades.

Then the bill's own sponsor said:

"This was never the intent of the bill."Rep. Steve Eliason, Newsweek, March 2026

The goal was to catch people a court has barred from buying alcohol. Utah prints a red NO ALCOHOL SALE banner on their licenses.

But the scanners the law required check your age. They aren't built to catch that banner. Staff still have to look at the card, the same check as before the law.

And the goal? It isn't written anywhere in the law. So nobody could check whether it worked.

By spring, the legislature had scaled it back.
